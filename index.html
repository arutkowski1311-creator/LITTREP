Understanding Your Procedure

Under continuous intraoperative MR thermometry, a NeuroBlate laser probe () was advanced along trajectory to the target and laser interstitial thermal therapy was delivered through a small cranial opening rather than an open resection.

Thermal dose was tracked in real time against thermal-damage-threshold (TDT) lines — the yellow and blue contours above, which represent accumulated thermal dose rather than instantaneous temperature (yellow ≈ 43°C for 2 minutes, blue ≈ 43°C for 10 minutes). The console recorded over across , with coverage driven to the higher-dose blue contour through the target before cooldown and probe position monitored continuously on the source frames.

What this reflects: an image-guided ablation executed with live temperature feedback at every pulse. The operator reads a real-time thermal map and steers dose to the planned margin rather than working blind. Clinical terms are clickable for a plain explanation.

On , your care team at performed Laser Interstitial Thermal Therapy (LITT).

LITT is a minimally invasive procedure that treats a targeted area within the brain using a thin laser fiber guided by MRI. Rather than performing traditional open brain surgery, the surgeon reaches the treatment area through a very small opening while continuously using MRI to guide the procedure and monitor the treatment in real time.

These images were recorded during your procedure. They are provided to help you better understand the treatment that was performed.

This report explains the procedure. It does not explain your results. Every patient's condition is different, and only your treating physician can interpret your imaging, discuss the outcome of your procedure, and explain what to expect next.
